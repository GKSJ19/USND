01
Milestone 1 · Data Quality

Dataset structure & validation

The dataset contains 46,185 federal disaster declarations spanning 1953–2017 across 57 U.S. states and territories. Date fields follow consistent MM/DD/YYYY formatting with zero missing values. Twenty disaster type categories exist; year and month were extracted for downstream analysis.

02
Milestone 2 · Temporal Trends

Time patterns and seasonality

Declarations grew from under 20/year in the 1950s to a peak of 4,736 in 2005. Three spikes: 1977 (winter storms), 1993 (Great Midwest Flood), 2005 (Katrina). September dominates seasonally with 8,281 declarations driven by Atlantic hurricane season.

03
Milestone 3 · Geographic Patterns

Spatial distribution and hotspots

Texas leads with 3,842 declarations — exposed to Gulf hurricanes, Plains storms, and western wildfires. Hurricane hotspots cluster along Gulf and Atlantic coasts. Flood hotspots span the Mississippi basin. Puerto Rico ranks 19th despite its small area.

04
Milestone 4 · Incident Analysis

Incident types and assistance programs

Storms account for 35% of all declarations. PA is activated in nearly every event. IH assistance concentrates in hurricanes (24%) and storms (31%). Drought and snow never trigger IH — they damage infrastructure, not homes.

∑
Unified Conclusion

Cross-milestone synthesis

U.S. disaster frequency has grown significantly since the 1990s, driven by storms and hurricanes. The South and Midwest bear the highest burden, with Texas and Louisiana as multi-hazard states. The Atlantic hurricane season (peak: September) creates the most concentrated annual period of declarations. Disaster risk is geographically concentrated and seasonally predictable — a strong empirical basis for targeted federal preparedness investment in the Gulf South and Central Plains during June–October.

About This Project

US Natural Disaster
Visualization Project

A milestone-based academic data visualization project analyzing FEMA federal disaster declarations from 1953 to 2017 using Python and modern web technologies.

The Dataset

The data comes from FEMA's OpenFEMA database — 46,185 federal disaster declarations spanning 65 years, covering all 50 U.S. states and several territories. Each record includes the declaration date, state, disaster type, and assistance program flags.

Project Structure

The project is structured across four milestones: data quality validation (M1), temporal trend analysis (M2), geographic distribution (M3), and incident type analysis (M4). Each milestone builds on the previous.

Tech Stack

Python Data processing — csv, json, collections, pathlib
Chart.js Bar, line, donut, stacked bar charts
D3.js Choropleth map with TopoJSON topology
HTML/CSS/JS Vanilla frontend, no frameworks needed

Key Findings

  • Storm declarations grew 8× from the 1980s to the 2000s
  • 2005 saw 4,736 declarations — an all-time peak
  • Texas leads all states with 3,842 declarations
  • September has 2.5× more declarations than the average month
  • Public Assistance is activated in nearly every disaster type
